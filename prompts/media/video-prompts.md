# Road Loop Production Prompts

## Purpose and source status

This file is a production brief for nine hero road loops and their posters for `1767:7102`. It does not claim that generated footage is documentary footage of the named roads. No video has been generated by writing this file.

The approved road labels come from Figma nodes `1767:7111-7119`. Road characteristics, exact geography, route geometry and links must be verified independently before publishing. If a loop is presented as a real named location, use commissioned or properly licensed documentary footage and record its source and rights in `docs/assets/asset-registry.md`.

Synthetic output may be used only as clearly registered atmospheric media. It cannot be evidence of a road, project, opening date, traffic condition or construction status.

## Deliverable specification for every loop

### Master

- One clean master per road, 3840 by 2160, 16:9, 25 fps, 10 seconds, progressive scan.
- Preferred mezzanine: ProRes 422 HQ or an equivalently high-quality intraframe master. If the generator supplies only a compressed file, retain the untouched original and record its codec, bitrate and checksum.
- No audio stream. Do not merely mute an embedded audio track.
- Natural shutter and motion blur. No frame interpolation artifacts.
- Stable horizon and restrained camera travel. Maximum perceived camera translation is approximately 5 percent of frame width across the loop.
- First and last frames must support a clean loop without a flash, horizon jump or reversed vehicle motion.

### Composition and safe areas

- Desktop: place the strongest road geometry in the right 55 percent. Keep the left 42 percent lower in detail and stable in luminance for approved copy and controls.
- Preserve a 6 percent edge safety zone. Do not place a unique vehicle, bridge pier or important road feature against a crop edge.
- Mobile 4:5: keep the primary vanishing point within the middle 50 percent. Preserve the upper 28 percent as a low-detail region where compact text may appear if the final component requires it.
- Do not bake text, labels, road shields, logos, arrows, UI, coordinates or a color wash into the media.
- Maintain a light, natural photographic grade. The page design supplies interface contrast; the footage must not simulate a dark-site theme.

### Loop construction

- Prefer a locked or very slowly drifting aerial, elevated roadside or stabilized tracking shot.
- Use traffic that can cross the seam without an obvious teleport. Avoid a distinctive vehicle entering in frame 1 and disappearing in frame 250.
- Do not use ping-pong playback because vehicles and construction machinery would reverse unnaturally.
- A 10 to 12 frame dissolve is allowed only when the horizon and traffic align and no ghost vehicles appear.
- If a clean seamless loop is impossible, use a deliberate cut on an occlusion, bridge pier, foreground tree or matched road marking. Reject a visible jump.
- Poster is selected from the calmest sharp frame, not necessarily frame 1. Poster and first decoded video frame must be color-matched to avoid a flash.

## Global generation prompt

Append this production language to each road-specific prompt:

> Premium naturalistic documentary-style motorway cinematography for a Russian public-infrastructure website. Realistic contemporary divided road engineering, lawful right-hand traffic, coherent lane markings, restrained traffic density, believable landscape scale, stable camera, subtle natural atmosphere, light editorial grade, no spectacle. Ten-second loop with a calm first frame suitable for a poster. Main road geometry sits primarily in the right half of a 16:9 frame, with a lower-detail left text-safe area. No baked text or interface.

### Global negative prompt

> No logos, no Autodor mark, no state symbols, no flags, no route shields, no readable road numbers, no license-plate text, no billboards, no watermarks, no subtitles, no UI, no decorative coordinates. No malformed lane markings, impossible interchanges, duplicated vehicles, vehicles facing the wrong direction, left-hand traffic, collisions, stopped vehicles in live lanes, unsafe workers, missing safety barriers, distorted bridges, invented road signs, unreadable sign text, dramatic emergency weather, cyberpunk lighting, purple or cyan grade, neon, oversaturated HDR, hyperlapse, speed ramp, whip pan, drone dive, aggressive tilt, lens dirt, heavy flare, miniature effect or time-lapse clouds.

The negative prompt is not a substitute for visual QA. Reject any frame with malformed signage, markings, vehicles, structures or unsafe construction behavior.

## Montage restrictions

1. Keep each road as one continuous shot. Do not cut together unrelated landscapes inside a 10-second loop.
2. Do not use speed ramps, drone dives, whip pans, crash zooms, match cuts through signs or aggressive transition effects.
3. Do not add route lines, maps, technical labels, gradients, logos or UI in the encoded media. Interface overlays belong to production code.
4. Do not stabilize so aggressively that vehicles or barriers warp at the frame edge.
5. Do not mirror a shot. Mirroring reverses traffic logic, signs and road geometry.
6. Do not reverse footage to build a loop.
7. Do not crop a 16:9 master automatically to mobile. Produce and approve a 4:5 art-directed crop for every road.
8. Do not apply a shared LUT without checking orange, asphalt, foliage, sky and skin tones against the page's light theme.
9. Keep exposure within a stable range. No white flash or black frame at the loop seam or between poster and video.
10. No visible text can be repaired by blur. Reject and regenerate or replace the source.
11. No synthetic road may be labeled in visible copy or alt text as a photographed named location.
12. Documentary and synthetic shots cannot be mixed without distinct registry provenance for every file.

## Poster extraction and crops

For each loop:

1. Review all 250 frames at 100 percent scale.
2. Reject frames with marking discontinuities, warped vehicles, unsafe road behavior, invalid structures, text artifacts or heavy compression.
3. Choose one sharp frame with the required desktop safe area and one independently composed mobile crop.
4. Export desktop AVIF and WebP plus mobile AVIF and WebP.
5. Compare poster to the first decoded video frame in the browser. Grade-match if the switch is visible.
6. Write factual, non-location-claiming alt text. Example: "A divided motorway crosses open fields at sunrise." Do not write "The M-12 near [place]" for synthetic media.

## Required registry fields before use

Add one row per road loop to `docs/assets/asset-registry.md` only after the file exists. Record:

- asset ID and approved road association;
- whether the source is commissioned, licensed documentary or synthetic;
- source URL, author, license and permission evidence where applicable;
- exact prompt, generator, model/version, seed or job ID and generation date for synthetic media;
- original filename, dimensions, duration, fps, codec, bitrate, file size and checksum;
- desktop and mobile derivatives with encoder settings and weights;
- poster paths and alt text;
- loop-seam, markings, traffic, structure, text artifact and compression QA status;
- final legal and editorial approval.

No registry row may say `generated` or `licensed` without evidence. A prompt is not an asset and cannot be marked complete.
